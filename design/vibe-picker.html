Warm Kitchen, refined

Same cozy direction, made calmer and less generic. The red is deeper and more like a ripe tomato, the cream is now a cleaner flour white, sage became olive, and the screen reads like a printed cookbook instead of a stack of cards. Pick a red below and every refined phone updates.

AI-look giveaways we are avoiding

Collected from designers' write-ups on what makes an interface look AI-generated, and how the app handles each one.

GiveawayWhat we do instead
Purple or blue gradients, glowing color blobs behind the heroFlat colors taken from food: tomato, olive, saffron. Real photos do the visual work.
Cream background + terracotta accent + trendy serif. This is now the "second" AI default, and the old Warm Kitchen was close to it.A whiter flour background, a deeper tomato red, olive instead of sage, and a chunky cookbook serif used only for recipe titles.
Inter, Geist or Plus Jakarta Sans everywhereYoung Serif for titles and Hanken Grotesk for everything else.
Everything inside identical rounded cards with soft shadows, sometimes cards inside cardsPlain lists with thin dividers, like a cookbook page. Cards only for recipe tiles in feeds.
Pill-shaped buttons everywhere, often with a trailing arrow ("Get started →")Rectangular buttons with a small radius and a plain verb ("Start cooking"). Pills only for filter chips.
Emoji used as icons or section headingsOne consistent icon set, used sparingly. No emoji in the interface.
Everything centered; rows of three equal cardsLeft-aligned text; feeds mix one large tile with smaller ones.
The standard font-size ladder (12/14/16/18/24…)Our own scale: 13 / 15 / 17 / 22 / 28.
Glassmorphism, neon glow, heavy shadows on every elementNo blur effects. Shadows only on things that float, like bottom sheets and the Cook Mode timer.
Placeholder content: "John Doe", lorem ipsum, stock icons for foodReal recipes with real units (g, ml, °C), real photos, real cooking times.
Sparkle icons and "AI-powered" badgesIf we add smart features, we name them by what they do: "Cook with what's in my fridge".

One more check: white text on the old red (#E4572E) fails the WCAG AA contrast minimum for normal text. All the new reds pass, so buttons are easier to read in a bright kitchen.
